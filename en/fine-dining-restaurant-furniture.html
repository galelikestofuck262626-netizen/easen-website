Elevate Your Fine Dining Experience

Fine dining furniture is more than functional seating — it's an integral part of the guest experience, setting the tone for a $150+ per cover meal. From the moment guests are seated, your furniture communicates luxury, attention to detail, and the promise of an exceptional evening. Whether you're opening a Michelin-starred restaurant, an upscale steakhouse, a luxury hotel dining room, or an intimate tasting menu venue, your furniture investment should reflect your culinary ambitions.

EASEN Furniture, located in Foshan, Guangdong Province — the heart of China's premium furniture manufacturing — specializes in fine dining furniture that balances elegant aesthetics, exceptional comfort, and uncompromising quality. We work with upscale restaurants, luxury hotels, and hospitality groups worldwide to create furniture that enhances their dining experience and brand prestige.

Fine Dining Seating

1. Upholstered Dining Chairs

The foundation of fine dining seating — elegant upholstered chairs that provide comfort, style, and flexibility.

Construction:

  • Frame: Solid hardwood (beech, oak, walnut, ash) with mortise-and-tenon joinery
  • Foam: High-density 2.5-3.0 lb/ft³, dual-density layers (soft top, firm base)
  • Suspension: Sinuous springs or webbing for seat support
  • Upholstery: Full-grain leather, velvet, mohair, or linen performance fabric
  • Finish: Hand-rubbed stain, French polish, or painted finish

Design options:

  • Back styles: Tight back, pillow back, channel tufted, button tufted, wingback
  • Arms: Armless (side chair) or with arms (more comfortable but takes more space)
  • Details: Nailhead trim, contrast piping, carved details, brass ferrules
  • Legs: Tapered, turned, cabriole, metal (brass or stainless steel)

Typical dimensions:

  • Seat height: 18-19" (standard dining height)
  • Seat depth: 20-22"
  • Seat width: 20-24"
  • Overall height: 36-40" (to backrest top)

Cost range: $250-500 per chair (FOB China)

2. Luxury Booth Seating

Privacy and intimacy — high-back booths create semi-private dining spaces, ideal for VIP guests and romantic dinners.

Construction:

  • Frame: Solid hardwood perimeter, plywood panels, corner blocking
  • Foam: High-density 2.5-3.0 lb/ft³, channel foam for tufting
  • Upholstery: Full-grain leather or luxury fabric
  • Backrest height: 42-54" (high-back for privacy)
  • Wood trim: Solid walnut, oak, or lacquered wood cap rail

Booth types:

  • Single-sided wall banquettes: Against wall, facing freestanding chairs across table
  • Back-to-back double booths: Two booths sharing a divider (space-efficient)
  • Curved booths: Semicircular banquettes around round tables (most intimate)
  • Corner booths: L-shaped for corner spaces
  • Freestanding booths: Stand-alone booth with high backs on three sides

Design details:

  • Deep button tufting (classic Chesterfield style)
  • Channel tufting (vertical lines, modern elegance)
  • Nailhead trim along seams and cap rail
  • Contrasting leather panels or piping
  • Carved wood details on cap rail

Cost range: $400-800 per linear foot (FOB China)

3. Lounge Seating (Bar & Waiting Area)

Luxurious pre-dining experience — comfortable lounge seating for bar areas and waiting lounges.

Seating types:

  • Lounge chairs: Deep-seated chairs (24-26" depth), low arms, 16-17" seat height
  • Club chairs: Traditional upholstered armchairs, barrel backs, nailhead trim
  • Sofas and loveseats: Two-seat or three-seat, for waiting areas
  • Chaise lounges: Elongated seating for ultra-luxury venues
  • Ottomans and poufs: Footrests or additional flexible seating

Cost range: $400-900 per lounge chair, $1,200-2,500 per sofa

4. Bar Stools (for Bar Seating)

Elevated dining experience — comfortable bar-height seating for chef's counter, sushi bar, or wine bar.

Construction for fine dining:

  • Frame: Solid hardwood or brushed stainless steel
  • Seat: Upholstered in leather or fabric, 16-18" diameter or square
  • Backrest: Full back (14-16" high) for comfort during long meals
  • Swivel: 360° swivel mechanism for ease of entry/exit
  • Footrest: Built-in foot rail, often wrapped in matching leather
  • Height: Counter (24-26") or bar height (28-30")

Cost range: $180-400 per stool

Table Sizes & Spacing

Fine dining requires generous table sizes and spacing for guest comfort and privacy:

Two-Top Tables

  • 30" × 30" square (minimum for fine dining)
  • 32" × 32" square (more comfortable)
  • 36" diameter round (optimal for intimate dining)
  • 24" × 36" rectangular (romantic, couples facing each other)

Spacing: Minimum 36" between tables, 42-48" preferred

Four-Top Tables

  • 36" × 36" square (minimum)
  • 42" × 42" square (comfortable)
  • 48" diameter round (optimal for conversation)
  • 54" diameter round (very spacious)

Spacing: Minimum 42" between tables, 48-54" preferred

Booth Tables

  • 30" × 54" (two-person booth)
  • 32" × 60" (standard two-person)
  • 36" × 72" (four-person booth)

Note: Wider tables (36") feel more luxurious and allow larger plate presentations

Private Dining Tables

  • 72" × 42" (seats 8)
  • 96" × 48" (seats 10-12)
  • 120" × 54" (seats 12-14)
  • Custom sizes for private dining rooms

Table Base Options

  • Cast iron pedestal: Heavy, stable, classic look, powder-coated or paint finish, $120-220 per base
  • Wood pedestal: Solid hardwood turned column, matches chairs, elegant, $150-280 per base
  • Brushed stainless steel: Modern, sleek, corrosion-resistant, $180-320 per base
  • Brass or gold-finished metal: Luxurious, art deco aesthetic, $220-400 per base
  • Four-leg wood table: Traditional craftsmanship, $300-600 per table (with top)

EASEN recommendation: For fine dining, choose table bases that are overbuilt — heavier and more robust than necessary. This eliminates any table wobble and communicates quality to guests.

Premium Materials for Fine Dining

Upholstery Materials

Full-Grain Leather

The gold standard for luxury seating — genuine leather develops a beautiful patina over time.

Advantages:

  • Luxurious feel and appearance
  • Breathes (comfortable for 2-3 hour meals)
  • Develops character with age (patina)
  • Long lifespan (10-15 years)
  • Easy to wipe clean (liquid spills don't absorb)

Considerations:

  • Higher cost ($40-80 per yard)
  • Can scratch or scuff (adds to patina)
  • Periodic conditioning needed (leather cream, 2-4 times/year)

Best for: Classic steakhouses, French bistros, traditional fine dining

Velvet & Mohair

Opulent and dramatic — plush pile fabrics that create a luxurious tactile experience.

Advantages:

  • Exceptionally soft, inviting texture
  • Rich color depth (jewel tones look stunning)
  • Sophisticated, elegant aesthetic
  • Available in performance versions (stain-resistant treatment)

Considerations:

  • Shows wear patterns (pile can flatten in high-use areas)
  • Requires professional cleaning (not wipeable like leather)
  • Performance velvet recommended over standard velvet (100,000+ double rubs)

Best for: Boutique restaurants, lounges, intimate tasting menu venues

Performance Linen & Wool Blends

Understated elegance — natural-looking fabrics with commercial-grade performance.

Advantages:

  • Sophisticated, refined appearance
  • Texture adds visual interest
  • Stain-resistant treatments available
  • Durable (80,000-100,000 double rubs)

Considerations:

  • Must be performance-treated for restaurants
  • Professional cleaning needed for deep stains
  • Shows soiling more than darker fabrics

Best for: Contemporary fine dining, farm-to-table concepts, coastal restaurants

Wood Species & Finishes

  • Walnut: Rich, dark brown with beautiful grain, most luxurious appearance, $$ per board foot
  • Oak (white or red): Classic, durable, prominent grain, versatile for staining, $ per board foot
  • Ash: Light color, similar to oak, more contemporary look, $ per board foot
  • Cherry: Warm reddish-brown, darkens with age, traditional elegance, $$ per board foot
  • Maple: Light, fine grain, modern aesthetic, takes stain well, $ per board foot

Wood Finish Options:

  • Hand-rubbed oil finish: Penetrates wood, natural feel, requires maintenance, develops patina
  • Catalyzed lacquer: Hard protective surface, chemical-resistant, low maintenance, commercial standard
  • French polish (shellac): Traditional technique, warm glow, labor-intensive, highest-end
  • Stain + polyurethane: Customizable color, durable clear coat, easy to maintain
  • Painted finish: Solid color, hides wood grain, unlimited colors, can be distressed

Hardware & Details

  • Nailhead trim: Individual brass or nickel nailheads along seams, classic detailing
  • Contrast piping: Fabric or leather piping in contrasting color, defines edges
  • Button tufting: Deep buttons create diamond or square patterns, Chesterfield style
  • Channel tufting: Vertical channels, modern elegance
  • Brass ferrules: Metal caps on chair/table legs, protects floor and adds detail

Custom Design Process

Fine dining restaurants often require custom furniture to achieve their unique aesthetic vision. EASEN's design and manufacturing process supports full customization:

What drives the cost difference between mid-range and high-end?

  • Materials: Performance fabric vs. genuine leather vs. exotic leather; wood veneer vs. solid walnut
  • Construction: Standard joinery vs. hand-fitted mortise-and-tenon; standard foam vs. dual-density premium foam
  • Finish quality: Standard spray lacquer vs. hand-rubbed finish vs. French polish
  • Details: Simple design vs. button tufting, nailhead trim, carved details, inlay work
  • Customization: Standard sizes vs. fully custom dimensions and design

How long does production take?

  • Design and sampling: 6-8 weeks
  • Production: 10-14 weeks after sample approval
  • Shipping: 4-6 weeks (sea freight) or 7-10 days (air freight)
  • Total timeline: 20-28 weeks (inquiry to delivery) for custom fine dining furniture

Is leather or fabric better for fine dining?

Leather advantages:

  • Wipes clean easily (important for red wine spills)
  • Develops beautiful patina over time
  • Classic, timeless look
  • Long lifespan (10-15 years)

Fabric advantages:

  • Softer, more inviting tactile experience
  • Wider range of colors and textures
  • Breathes better (cooler in warm environments)
  • Can achieve specific aesthetic (velvet opulence, linen casualness)

EASEN recommendation: For chairs that will be heavily used (main dining), leather is more practical. For booths and lounge seating (less direct contact, more visual impact), luxury fabrics like velvet or mohair create dramatic atmosphere.

How comfortable should fine dining seating be?

Fine dining seating must support 2-3 hour meals comfortably:

  • Foam density: 2.5-3.0 lb/ft³ (plush but supportive) — denser than casual dining but not so firm it feels hard
  • Seat depth: 20-22" (deep enough to sit back comfortably)
  • Backrest: 18-20" high minimum, with lumbar support curve
  • Seat slope: Slight backward slope (2-3°) encourages relaxed posture
  • Armrests: Optional, but increase comfort significantly (allow 6-8" more width per chair)

Test: Have someone sit in sample chair for 20-30 minutes. If comfortable for that duration, it will work for fine dining.

What warranty do you provide?

2-year commercial warranty covering:

  • Structural failures (frame breakage, joint separation)
  • Manufacturing defects (finish issues, hardware failures, foam compression beyond normal)
  • Material defects (wood splitting, laminate delamination)

Warranty excludes normal wear and tear, guest damage, and improper maintenance. For valid warranty claims, we ship replacement parts or furniture at our expense.
