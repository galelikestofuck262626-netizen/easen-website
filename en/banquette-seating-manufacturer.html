Custom Banquette Seating from China

Banquette seating — also called built-in seating, fixed seating, or wall banquettes — is a space-efficient, high-capacity seating solution for restaurants, cafes, and hotels. Unlike standalone booth seating, banquettes are typically fixed along walls or used as room dividers, maximizing seating density while creating a cohesive, upscale aesthetic.

EASEN Furniture, based in Foshan, Guangdong Province, specializes in manufacturing custom commercial banquette seating for the global hospitality market. Our banquettes are engineered for durability, comfort, and style — designed to withstand heavy daily use in high-traffic dining environments.

Types of Banquette Seating We Manufacture

EASEN produces a full range of banquette configurations to suit different restaurant layouts, seating capacities, and design concepts:

1. Straight Wall Banquettes

The most common type — a continuous upholstered bench fixed along a wall. Ideal for maximizing capacity along perimeter walls.

Typical specs:

  • Seat depth: 18-24 inches
  • Seat height: 18-19 inches
  • Back height: 18-42 inches
  • Length: Custom (usually 4-20 feet per section)

Best for: Casual dining, cafes, fast-casual restaurants

2. L-Shaped Corner Banquettes

Two straight banquette runs joined at a 90-degree angle, creating a corner seating area. Maximizes corner space and creates intimate seating zones.

Design considerations:

  • Corner piece can be square, curved, or angled
  • Each leg typically 4-8 feet
  • Requires structural support at the corner

Best for: Boutique restaurants, wine bars, bistros

3. U-Shaped Banquettes

Three-sided seating creating a semi-private dining alcove. Often paired with a single large table to seat 6-10 guests.

Configuration:

  • Back wall: 5-8 feet
  • Side walls: 3-5 feet each
  • Can be fully upholstered or open-ended

Best for: Fine dining, private dining rooms, VIP sections

4. Curved Banquettes

Banquette seating following a curved wall or creating a circular/oval seating area. More complex to manufacture but creates dramatic visual impact.

Technical requirements:

  • Requires precise radius measurements
  • Frame built on curved laminated plywood base
  • Upholstery must be carefully templated

Best for: Upscale restaurants, hotel lobbies, statement seating areas

5. Back-to-Back Banquettes

Two banquettes placed back-to-back, functioning as a room divider or creating two-sided seating rows. Space-efficient for open floor plans.

Construction:

  • Shared structural frame in the middle
  • Upholstered on both sides
  • Total depth: 32-48 inches
  • Can be freestanding or anchored to floor

Best for: Large dining rooms, food halls, cafeterias

6. Freestanding Banquettes

Not attached to walls — standalone banquette units that can be positioned anywhere in the space. More flexible but require structural base for stability.

Features:

  • Requires robust internal frame
  • Often includes base platform or legs
  • Can be moved (though heavy)
  • Back may be upholstered on both sides for visual appeal

Best for: Flexible seating areas, event spaces, hotel restaurants

Banquette Construction & Materials

EASEN's commercial banquettes are built to withstand years of heavy restaurant use. Here's how we construct them:

Frame Construction

The foundation of durable banquette seating is a properly engineered frame:

  • Base frame: Kiln-dried hardwood (oak, beech, or birch) or plywood box construction
  • Joinery: Mortise-and-tenon joints, reinforced with corner blocks and wood glue
  • Wall attachment: Metal L-brackets or French cleats for secure wall mounting (built-in banquettes)
  • Freestanding base: Steel tube legs or solid plywood base platform (freestanding banquettes)
  • Back support: Plywood back panel (12-18mm) provides structural integrity and upholstery substrate

Cushioning & Foam

Comfort and longevity depend on proper cushioning specifications:

  • Seat foam: High-density polyurethane foam, minimum 2.2 lbs/ft³ density, 35-40 ILD (Indentation Load Deflection)
  • Back foam: Medium-density foam, 1.8-2.0 lbs/ft³ density, 25-30 ILD for comfort without excessive softness
  • Foam thickness: 3-4 inches for seats, 2-3 inches for backs
  • Fire rating: CA117 compliant foam for California and most U.S. jurisdictions
  • Dacron wrap: 1/2-inch polyester fiber wrap over foam prevents upholstery abrasion and provides rounded edge appearance

Upholstery Options

We offer three primary upholstery categories for commercial banquettes:

1. Commercial Vinyl (Most Popular for Restaurants)

  • Durability: 500,000+ double rubs (Wyzenbeek test) — suitable for heavy restaurant use
  • Cleanability: Easy to wipe down with disinfectant — critical for foodservice
  • Flame retardancy: Meets CA117, NFPA 260, and UK FMVSS302 standards
  • Appearance: Available in smooth, grain, and textured finishes in 200+ colors
  • Cost: Most economical option — $8-15 per yard
  • Best for: Casual dining, fast-casual, high-turnover restaurants

2. Top-Grain Leather (Premium Option)

  • Durability: Natural hide with protective finish — ages gracefully with use
  • Cleanability: Requires leather-specific cleaners; more maintenance than vinyl
  • Appearance: Luxurious, authentic texture — cannot be replicated by vinyl
  • Cost: $25-50+ per square foot depending on grade
  • Best for: Fine dining, steakhouses, upscale hotel restaurants, wine bars

3. Performance Fabric (Design-Forward Option)

  • Durability: 100,000+ double rubs for commercial-grade fabrics
  • Cleanability: Solution-dyed or treated fabrics resist stains and moisture
  • Appearance: Soft hand-feel and wide variety of patterns, textures, and colors
  • Cost: $15-35 per yard depending on fabric quality
  • Best for: Cafes, boutique restaurants, hotels where aesthetics are paramount

Finishing Details

Small details that elevate the quality and appearance of custom banquettes:

  • Welt cord (piping): Contrasting or matching piping along seams for visual definition and seam reinforcement
  • Button tufting: Classic tufted back panels — diamond or biscuit tufting patterns available
  • Channel tufting: Vertical channels create a modern, streamlined look
  • Wood trim: Exposed wood cap rail or base trim in stained hardwood or painted finish
  • Metal trim: Brushed brass, polished chrome, or matte black metal accents
  • Under-seat storage: Hinged seat lifts for storage compartments (optional)

Design Considerations for Restaurant Banquettes

Effective banquette design balances comfort, capacity, and aesthetics. Here are key considerations:

Ergonomics & Comfort

Dimension Standard Range Notes
Seat Height 18-19 inches 18" is standard for dining; match table height (table surface should be 10-12" above seat)
Seat Depth 18-24 inches 20-22" is most comfortable; shallower for tight spaces, deeper for lounge-style seating
Back Height 18-42 inches Low back (18-24") for open feel; high back (36-42") for privacy and sound absorption
Table Clearance 6-8 inches Distance from banquette edge to table edge; 6" minimum for ease of entry
Aisle Width 24-36 inches Minimum 24" for single-sided access; 36"+ for main aisles with server traffic

Capacity Planning

How many diners can you seat per linear foot of banquette?

  • Tight seating: 1 person per 20 inches (3 people per 5 feet)
  • Standard seating: 1 person per 24 inches (2.5 people per 5 feet)
  • Comfortable seating: 1 person per 30 inches (2 people per 5 feet)

Note: Most restaurants use 24-inch spacing (standard) as the baseline for capacity calculations.

Style & Aesthetic Options

Traditional / Classic

  • Button tufting on back
  • Exposed wood trim cap rail
  • Leather or leather-look vinyl
  • Nailhead trim detailing
  • Rich, warm color palette (burgundy, forest green, cognac brown)

Best for: Steakhouses, taverns, classic diners, traditional bistros

Modern / Contemporary

  • Clean lines, no tufting
  • Channel stitching or smooth upholstery
  • Bold, saturated colors or neutrals
  • Metal base or floating appearance
  • Minimalist wood trim or no visible wood

Best for: Modern cafes, fast-casual chains, contemporary restaurants

Rustic / Industrial

  • Distressed leather or rugged vinyl
  • Exposed wood frame with reclaimed appearance
  • Metal brackets or visible hardware
  • Earth tones (tan, brown, gray, olive)

Best for: Brewpubs, gastropubs, farm-to-table restaurants

Upscale / Luxury

  • Premium top-grain leather
  • Deep button or diamond tufting
  • High back panels (36-42")
  • Polished brass or chrome accents
  • Jewel tones or sophisticated neutrals

Best for: Fine dining, hotel signature restaurants, wine bars

Banquette Seating FAQ

What's the difference between a booth and a banquette?

Booth seating is a complete unit — a table surrounded by upholstered benches on two or more sides. Booths are freestanding and can be moved (though they're heavy). Banquette seating refers only to the upholstered bench portion, typically fixed to a wall or used as a room divider. Banquettes are paired with separate tables and chairs/benches opposite.

In practice, the terms are sometimes used interchangeably in the restaurant industry.

Can banquettes be removed or repositioned later?

It depends on the installation method:

  • Wall-mounted banquettes: These are screwed or bracketed to the wall. They can be removed, but it's not trivial — expect to patch and repaint walls afterward.
  • Freestanding banquettes: These rest on their own base and can be moved relatively easily (though they may weigh 100-300+ lbs depending on size).

If you anticipate frequent layout changes, consider freestanding banquettes or modular booth units instead of fixed wall banquettes.

What's the minimum order quantity?

For custom banquette seating, our MOQ is typically 20-30 linear feet (about one small restaurant or cafe). For larger standard configurations or repeat orders, we can accommodate smaller quantities. Contact us with your project size for a specific MOQ.

Can you make banquettes to fit curved walls?

Yes. Curved banquettes require precise radius measurements and templating. The manufacturing process is more complex (curved frame, carefully patterned upholstery), so curved banquettes cost approximately 30-50% more than straight runs of equivalent length. Provide us with the radius or send a dimensioned drawing of the curve.

How do I clean and maintain commercial banquette seating?

Vinyl upholstery:

  • Daily: Wipe down with damp cloth and mild dish soap
  • Weekly: Use commercial vinyl cleaner for deeper cleaning
  • Spills: Clean immediately to prevent staining (though most commercial vinyl is stain-resistant)

Leather upholstery:

  • Weekly: Wipe with leather-specific cleaner
  • Monthly: Apply leather conditioner to prevent drying and cracking
  • Avoid harsh chemicals, bleach, or abrasive cleaners

Fabric upholstery:

  • Weekly: Vacuum with upholstery attachment
  • Stains: Blot immediately; use fabric-safe spot cleaner
  • Annually: Professional steam cleaning recommended

Do banquettes include under-seat storage?

Under-seat storage is available as an optional feature. We can build hinged seat tops that lift to reveal storage compartments underneath — useful for storing extra napkins, condiments, or high chairs. This adds approximately $50-100 per linear foot to the cost, depending on hardware quality.

Can you match existing furniture in my restaurant?

Yes. Send us clear photos of your existing furniture, ideally with a color swatch or fabric sample. We'll do our best to match the upholstery color, texture, and finish. For wood trim, send us a wood species and stain sample if possible. Keep in mind that exact color matching can be challenging (dye lot variations, aging), so slight differences may occur.

What's the typical lifespan of commercial banquette seating?

With proper construction and materials, commercial banquettes should last 7-12 years in a high-traffic restaurant environment. Factors affecting lifespan:

  • Frame quality: Solid hardwood frames last longer than plywood or particleboard
  • Foam density: High-density foam maintains its shape for 8-10 years; low-density foam sags in 3-5 years
  • Upholstery durability: Commercial-grade vinyl lasts longer than residential-grade; leather develops patina but can last 15+ years
  • Maintenance: Regular cleaning extends upholstery life significantly

Banquettes in lower-traffic cafes or hotel lobbies can last 15+ years. High-turnover fast-casual restaurants may need replacement in 5-7 years.

How much weight can banquette seating hold?

EASEN's commercial banquettes are engineered to support 300-350 lbs per linear foot of distributed load. For example, a 6-foot straight banquette can safely hold approximately 1,800-2,100 lbs total — comfortably seating 3-4 adults with a significant safety margin.

This assumes proper wall mounting (for built-in banquettes) or a robust base frame (for freestanding banquettes).

Do you offer flame-retardant upholstery?

Yes. All of our upholstery options can be manufactured with flame-retardant materials meeting:

  • California TB117-2013 (updated flammability standard)
  • NFPA 260 (National Fire Protection Association)
  • UK FMVSS302 (for UK and European projects)

Specify your jurisdiction's requirements when ordering, and we'll ensure compliance.
