Outdoor Restaurant Chairs

Outdoor chairs must be lightweight enough for guests and staff to move, durable enough to resist weather, and stackable for efficient storage. EASEN manufactures all common outdoor chair types:

Aluminum Dining Chairs

The most popular choice for commercial outdoor dining — lightweight, rustproof, and durable.

Construction:

  • Welded aluminum tube frame (1.2-1.5mm wall thickness)
  • Powder-coat finish in any color (UV-stable, fade-resistant)
  • Optional: sling seat, plastic seat, wood-look slats

Benefits:

  • Lightweight (5-8 lbs per chair) — easy to rearrange
  • 100% rustproof — ideal for coastal locations
  • Stackable (8-10 chairs high)
  • UV-resistant finish won't fade or chalk

Lifespan: 7-10 years in full outdoor exposure

Powder-Coated Steel Chairs

Heavier and more stable than aluminum — classic bistro chair aesthetic.

Construction:

  • Welded steel tube frame (16-18 gauge)
  • Multi-layer powder-coat finish
  • Heavier weight (12-18 lbs) resists wind tipping

Benefits:

  • Traditional cafe aesthetic (Parisian bistro style)
  • Very stable in wind
  • Lower cost than aluminum
  • Stackable

Consideration: Can rust if powder-coat is chipped; requires touch-up maintenance. Best for dry climates or covered patios.

Plastic (Polypropylene) Chairs

Most economical option — lightweight, stackable, and easy to clean.

Construction:

  • Injection-molded polypropylene
  • UV-stabilized resin (prevents brittleness)
  • Available in solid colors or transparent

Benefits:

  • Very lightweight (4-6 lbs)
  • Lowest cost option
  • Easy to clean (hose down)
  • High stackability (15-20 chairs high)

Best for: Casual cafes, quick-service restaurants, poolside dining, budget-conscious operations

Lifespan: 3-5 years; color may fade in extreme sun

Folding Chairs

Space-saving design — fold flat for compact storage.

Best for:

  • Restaurants that must store furniture nightly (city regulations)
  • Seasonal outdoor dining (summer-only patios)
  • Event spaces with flexible seating needs

Materials: Aluminum or steel frame with sling, plastic, or wood slat seat

Trade-off: Folding hinges are a wear point; not as durable as non-folding designs

Synthetic Wicker Chairs

Resort-style aesthetic with weather-resistant resin wicker.

Construction:

  • Powder-coated aluminum frame
  • UV-resistant PE (polyethylene) wicker weave
  • Optional cushions with outdoor-rated fabric

Benefits:

  • Upscale, resort aesthetic
  • Comfortable with cushions
  • Weather-resistant (won't rot like natural wicker)

Best for: Hotel patios, resort restaurants, upscale waterfront dining

Cost: Premium option (2-3x cost of basic aluminum)

Teak Wood Chairs

Natural teak wood — the gold standard for outdoor furniture durability.

Benefits:

  • Naturally weather-resistant (high oil content prevents rot)
  • Beautiful warm patina (ages to silver-gray if left untreated)
  • Extremely durable (15-20 year lifespan)
  • Upscale aesthetic

Maintenance: Optional oil treatment 1-2x/year to maintain golden color; otherwise no maintenance required

Cost: Premium to luxury option (3-5x cost of aluminum)

Best for: High-end restaurants, resort dining, coastal properties

Outdoor Restaurant Tables

Outdoor table tops must resist warping, fading, and staining, while bases must be stable enough to resist tipping in wind. EASEN manufactures complete outdoor table systems:

Outdoor Table Top Options

1. Compact Laminate (HPL Exterior Grade)

Most popular choice for commercial outdoor dining — durable, affordable, and low-maintenance.

  • Construction: 12-13mm solid laminate core (no wood substrate to swell)
  • Finish: Both sides laminated for outdoor use; 100+ wood grains, solid colors, marble looks
  • Durability: UV-stable, waterproof, heat-resistant to 350°F, scratch-resistant
  • Maintenance: Wipe clean with damp cloth; no sealing or re-oiling required
  • Lifespan: 7-10 years in full outdoor exposure
  • Cost: Moderate ($$)

2. Aluminum Composite

Lightweight metal table tops — modern aesthetic, extremely durable.

  • Construction: Aluminum honeycomb core + aluminum or HPL face sheets
  • Weight: Very lightweight — easier for staff to move
  • Finish: Powder-coat, anodized, or brushed aluminum
  • Best for: Modern/contemporary restaurants, rooftop bars, urban patios
  • Cost: Moderate to premium ($$ - $$$)

3. Teak Wood

Premium natural wood — warm aesthetic and exceptional durability.

  • Construction: Solid teak slats or planks; marine-grade stainless steel hardware
  • Finish: Natural oil finish or left untreated to weather to silver-gray patina
  • Maintenance: Optional oil treatment 1-2x/year; otherwise no maintenance
  • Best for: Upscale restaurants, resort dining, coastal properties
  • Cost: Premium to luxury ($$$$ )

4. Granite & Stone

Luxurious and statement-making — extremely heavy and durable.

  • Materials: Natural granite, marble, or engineered stone
  • Weight: Very heavy (150-300 lbs per table) — excellent wind stability
  • Maintenance: Requires periodic sealing (marble is porous and stains)
  • Best for: Upscale restaurants, hotel terraces, destinations with mild climates
  • Cost: Luxury ($$$$)

5. Ceramic Tile

Mosaic or large-format tile table tops — decorative and durable.

  • Construction: Ceramic or porcelain tiles set in concrete or marine-grade substrate
  • Appearance: Colorful patterns, Mediterranean aesthetic
  • Durability: Very durable; grout lines may require periodic resealing
  • Best for: Mediterranean, Spanish, or Mexican restaurants; resort properties

Climate Considerations

Coastal / Saltwater Environments

  • Best materials: Aluminum, teak, synthetic wicker, stainless steel hardware
  • Avoid: Untreated steel (will rust quickly from salt air)
  • Maintenance: Rinse furniture weekly with fresh water to remove salt deposits

Desert / High UV Environments

  • Best materials: Powder-coated aluminum or steel, compact laminate, UV-stabilized plastics
  • Avoid: Untreated wood (dries out and cracks), low-quality plastics (become brittle)
  • Maintenance: Use high-quality powder-coat with UV inhibitors

Rainy / Humid Climates

  • Best materials: Aluminum, teak, compact laminate, synthetic wicker
  • Avoid: Untreated wood (rots), steel without drainage holes (rust), cushions without quick-dry foam
  • Design tip: Sloped seat surfaces and drainage holes prevent water pooling

Cold / Freezing Climates

  • Best materials: Aluminum, steel, compact laminate
  • Avoid: Plastics (can crack in extreme cold), cushions (absorb moisture that freezes)
  • Best practice: Store furniture indoors during winter months

Outdoor Furniture Design Considerations

Successful outdoor dining spaces balance aesthetics, comfort, durability, and operational efficiency. Here are key design principles:

1. Wind Resistance & Stability

Wind is the #1 cause of outdoor furniture damage and safety incidents. Design for stability:

  • Heavy bases: 50+ lbs for tables up to 36", 70+ lbs for larger tables
  • Low center of gravity: Wide, flat base plates distribute weight
  • Chair weight: Heavier chairs (10-15 lbs) less likely to blow over when empty
  • Umbrella wind vents: Prevent umbrellas from acting as sails in wind
  • Anchoring options: Bolt-down bases for rooftops and high-wind areas

2. Drainage & Water Management

Standing water causes rust, mold, and discomfort. Design for drainage:

  • Slatted seats: Gaps between slats allow water to drain
  • Perforated seats: Small holes in solid seats prevent pooling
  • Tilted surfaces: Slight slope sheds water
  • Open-frame designs: Minimize horizontal surfaces where water can collect

3. Stackability & Storage

Off-season or bad-weather storage is critical for maximizing furniture lifespan:

  • Stackable chairs: 8-10 high (aluminum/plastic) or 4-6 high (heavier designs)
  • Nesting tables: Smaller tables nest under larger ones
  • Folding furniture: Folds flat for compact storage
  • Storage space calculation: 10 stackable chairs = ~4 sq ft floor space vs. 40+ sq ft when set up

4. Comfort & Ergonomics

Outdoor furniture must balance weather resistance with comfort:

  • Contoured seats: Molded shapes more comfortable than flat surfaces
  • Armrests: Add comfort but reduce seating capacity (chairs take up more space)
  • Cushions: Optional cushions add comfort; use quick-dry foam and UV-resistant outdoor fabrics
  • Back angle: 100-110° recline angle comfortable for 1-2 hour dining
  • Seat height: 17-18" standard dining height

5. Space Planning for Outdoor Dining

Outdoor spaces often feel more spacious but require same clearances as indoor dining:

  • Table spacing: Minimum 24" between chair backs (36" preferred for comfortable aisle)
  • Service aisles: 36-42" wide for servers carrying trays
  • Exit paths: Maintain clear 44" exit paths (ADA requirement in USA)
  • Perimeter clearance: 18-24" buffer from walls, railings, and landscaping
  • Capacity calculation: Allow 10-12 sq ft per guest for comfortable outdoor dining (vs. 12-15 sq ft indoors)

6. Color & Finish Selection

Color choices affect appearance longevity and heat absorption:

  • Dark colors (black, bronze, charcoal): Absorb heat (can be uncomfortably hot in direct sun); hide dirt and wear; timeless aesthetic
  • Light colors (white, cream, light gray): Reflect heat (cooler to touch); show dirt more easily; bright, fresh aesthetic
  • Earth tones (taupe, olive, terracotta): Balance of heat management and dirt hiding; natural aesthetic
  • Bold colors (red, blue, green): Statement-making; may fade faster in intense UV; brand identity opportunity

Outdoor Furniture FAQ

What is the typical lead time for outdoor restaurant furniture?

Standard production: 6-8 weeks from order confirmation to ex-factory
With pre-production sample: 2-3 weeks for sample + 6-8 weeks for production = 8-11 weeks total
Rush orders: 4-5 week expedited production available (surcharge applies)
Shipping: Add 4-6 weeks for sea freight or 7-10 days for air freight

What is the minimum order quantity?

MOQ varies by furniture type and material:

  • Aluminum chairs: 100 chairs per style/color
  • Plastic chairs: 200 chairs per color (due to injection molding setup costs)
  • Outdoor tables: 30 tables per size/finish
  • Complete outdoor furniture packages: For orders including chairs + tables, we can accommodate lower per-item MOQs (e.g., 60 chairs + 20 tables)

How long does outdoor furniture last?

Lifespan depends on material, climate, and maintenance:

Should I store outdoor furniture during winter?

Recommended for:

  • Locations with freezing temperatures and snow
  • Areas with 3+ months of rainy season
  • Seasonal restaurants closed during winter

Benefits of winter storage: Doubles furniture lifespan, prevents rust and UV damage, reduces maintenance

If storage isn't available: Cover furniture with waterproof covers or use furniture designed for year-round exposure (aluminum, teak, compact laminate)

Can outdoor furniture be used indoors?

Yes, outdoor-rated furniture can be used indoors with no issues. Many restaurants use outdoor-style aluminum or plastic chairs indoors for:

  • Industrial/modern aesthetic
  • Easy cleaning (hose-down or wipe-clean)
  • Flexible indoor/outdoor use (move furniture between spaces as needed)
  • Durability (outdoor furniture is built tougher than many indoor pieces)

What's the difference between "outdoor-rated" and "all-weather" furniture?

The terms are often used interchangeably, but technically:

  • Outdoor-rated: Designed for outdoor use but benefits from seasonal storage or covers during harsh weather
  • All-weather: Built for year-round outdoor exposure with no storage required (aluminum, teak, compact laminate)

For commercial restaurants, specify commercial-grade outdoor furniture — residential-grade outdoor furniture won't hold up to daily restaurant use.

How do I prevent outdoor furniture from fading?

UV exposure causes all materials to fade over time. To minimize fading:

  • Choose UV-stabilized materials: High-quality powder-coat, UV-stabilized plastics, compact laminate with UV topcoat
  • Select fade-resistant colors: Earth tones and neutrals fade less noticeably than bright colors
  • Use shade: Umbrellas, pergolas, or awnings reduce direct UV exposure by 70-90%
  • Seasonal storage: Store furniture during off-season to reduce UV exposure hours
  • Quality matters: Premium powder-coat finishes and materials fade 50% slower than budget options

Can you manufacture furniture in custom colors?

Yes. For powder-coated aluminum and steel furniture, we can match any RAL color or provide custom color matching from your sample. Custom colors typically require:

  • Minimum order quantity: 100+ pieces per custom color
  • Lead time: Add 1-2 weeks for custom powder-coat setup
  • Cost: Small upcharge ($1-3 per piece) for non-standard colors

Do you offer replacement parts?

  • Table bases and base plates
  • Chair seats and backs
  • Furniture glides and levelers

Tip: Order 10-15% extra furniture or parts initially to keep as spares for future replacements.

EASEN provides a 2-year commercial warranty covering:

  • Manufacturing defects in materials or workmanship

Warranty excludes normal wear and tear, misuse, damage from extreme weather events (hurricanes, hail), and improper maintenance.
